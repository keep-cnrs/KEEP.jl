C Estimate of the eight length

Let \(D:=2\Delta\theta/\Delta\phi\), \(s:=\sin\theta_0\), \(\alpha:=2\Delta\theta=D\,\Delta\phi\), \(\beta:=\Delta\phi\,s\); recall that \[L_{81}=\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{[2\Delta\theta\cos2\tau]^2+\sin^2(\theta_0+\Delta\theta\sin2\tau)\,[\Delta\phi\cos\tau]^2}\,\dd\tau,\] and let \(\widetilde L_{81}\) be the quantity obtained by replacing \(\sin^2(\theta_0+\Delta\theta\sin2\tau)\) by \(\sin^2\theta_0\): \[\widetilde L_{81}:=\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{\alpha^2\cos^22\tau+\beta^2\cos^2\tau}\,\dd\tau .\] The error of this approximation is analysed as follows, with \(x:=\Delta\theta\) and \(E(x):=L_{81}-\widetilde L_{81}\). Decoupling the coefficient of \(\cos2\tau\) from the amplitude in the sine, put, for \(a\in\mathbf{R}\) and \(\varepsilon\) small (only \(a^2\) enters, so \(\Lambda\) is even in \(a\)), \[\Lambda(a,\varepsilon):=\int_0^{2\pi}\sqrt{a^2\cos^22\tau+\Delta\phi^2\cos^2\tau\,\sin^2(\theta_0+\varepsilon\sin2\tau)}\,\dd\tau,\] so that \(L_{81}=\Lambda(2x,x)\), \(\widetilde L_{81}=\Lambda(2x,0)\) and \(E(x)=\Lambda(2x,x)-\Lambda(2x,0)\). Then \(E\) is twice differentiable at \(0\), with \(E(0)=0\) and \(E'(0)=0\), the latter because \(\partial_\varepsilon\Lambda(a,0)=0\) for every \(a\in\mathbf{R}\) (the integrand of \(\partial_\varepsilon\Lambda(a,0)\) is odd under \(\tau\mapsto\pi-\tau\) whereas the radicand is even), and \(E''(0)=\Lambda_{\varepsilon\varepsilon}(0,0)\). The latter is computable in closed form because at \(a=0\) the integrand collapses to \(\Delta\phi\,|\cos\tau|\sin(\theta_0+\varepsilon\sin2\tau)\), whose second derivative at \(\varepsilon=0\) is \(-\Delta\phi\,s\,|\cos\tau|\sin^22\tau\), whence \[E''(0)=-\Delta\phi\,s\int_0^{2\pi}|\cos\tau|\sin^22\tau\,\dd\tau=-\frac{32}{15}\,\Delta\phi\,s\ne0 .\] The Taylor–Young formula \(E(x)=\tfrac12E''(0)x^2+o(x^2)\) therefore gives \[L_{81}=\widetilde L_{81}-\frac{16}{15}\,\Delta\phi\,s\,\Delta\theta^2+o(\Delta\theta^2),\] and the approximation is of order exactly two. Set \[C:=\frac{\alpha^2}{\alpha^2+\beta^2}=\frac{D^2}{D^2+s^2}\in(0,1).\] By the symmetries of the integrand, and with \(S:=\sin^2\tau\) (so \(\dd S=2\sqrt{S(1-S)}\,\dd\tau\) and \(\cos2\tau=1-2S\)), \[\begin{gathered}\widetilde L_{81}=4\alpha\int_0^1\frac{N(S)\,\dd S}{w},\qquad w^2:=S(1-S)\,N(S),\\ N(S):=S^2-\sigma S+\varpi,\qquad \sigma:=\frac{1+3C}{4C},\qquad \varpi:=\frac1{4C},\end{gathered}\] where \(N>0\) on \([0,1]\), since \(N(0)=\varpi\) and \(N(1)=1/4\). The curve \(w^2=S(1-S)N(S)\) is a quartic with four simple roots for \(C\ne1/9\), hence of genus one, and the limits \(0\) and \(1\) are two of its roots, so \(\widetilde L_{81}\) is a complete elliptic integral. Sending the root \(S=0\) to infinity with \(t:=1/S\), and removing the quadratic term with \[x:=\frac{t-\tfrac13(2+3C)}{16\,C},\qquad y:=\frac{w}{16\,C\,S^2},\] the curve becomes \(y^2=4x^3-g_2x-g_3\) with \[g_2:=\frac{9C^2-9C+1}{192\,C^2},\qquad g_3:=\frac{(3C-1)(18C^2-21C+2)}{27648\,C^3},\qquad g_2^3-27g_3^2=\frac{(C-1)(9C-1)}{1048576\,C^4},\] which are rational in \(C\), hence in \(D^2\) and \(s^2\). The root \(S=1\) becomes \(x=e_r:=(1-3C)/(48C)\), and the point \(S=\infty\) becomes \(x=x_0:=-(2+3C)/(48C)\). Let \(\wp\) be the Weierstrass function of \((g_2,g_3)\), with period lattice generated by \(2\omega\) and \(2\omega'\), and let \(\zeta\) be defined by \(\zeta'=-\wp\) and \(\zeta(z)-1/z\to0\) as \(z\to0\), so that \(\zeta(z+2\omega)=\zeta(z)+2\eta\). Parametrising \(x=\wp(u)\), \(y=-\wp'(u)\), one has \(\dd S/w=\dd u\), and \(S\in[0,1]\) corresponds to \(u\in[0,\omega]\), where \[\begin{gathered}\omega:=\int_{e_r}^{\infty}\frac{\dd x}{\sqrt{4x^3-g_2x-g_3}},\qquad \eta:=\zeta(\omega),\\ b:=\int_{-\infty}^{x_0}\frac{\dd x}{\sqrt{g_3+g_2x-4x^3}},\qquad a:=-ib,\end{gathered}\] so that \(\omega\) is the real half-period (\(\wp(\omega)=e_r\) and \(\wp'(\omega)=0\)), and \(\wp(a)=x_0\), \(\wp'(a)=i/(16C)\). The lattice is rectangular iff \(g_2^3-27g_3^2>0\), i.e. iff \(C<1/9\) (equivalently \(s>2\sqrt2\,D\)), in which case \(\omega'\) is purely imaginary. It is rhombic (non-rectangular) for \(1/9<C<1\), and degenerate for \(C=1/9\). In terms of \(u\), with \(t=16C(\wp(u)-x_0)\), \[\frac{N\,\dd S}{w}=\Bigl(\varpi-\frac{\sigma}{t}+\frac1{t^2}\Bigr)\dd u,\] and the classical identity \(\wp'(a)/(\wp(u)-\wp(a))=\zeta(u-a)-\zeta(u+a)+2\zeta(a)\) gives \[\int_0^\omega\frac{\dd u}{\wp(u)-\wp(a)}=\frac{2\bigl(\omega\,\zeta(a)-\eta\,a\bigr)-i\pi}{\wp'(a)},\] where the constant \(-i\pi\) is the branch fixed by the behaviour for \(b\to0^+\), and the integral with a squared denominator is obtained by differentiating with respect to \(a\), using \(\wp'(a)^2=-1/(256C^2)\) and \(\wp''(a)=(1+7C)/(128C^2)\). Collecting the three kinds of terms, only \(\omega\), \(\eta\) and \(\zeta(a)\) remain: \[\widetilde L_{81}=4\alpha\,I(C),\qquad I(C):=\frac{(4-3C)\,\omega}{24\,C}+2\eta+\frac{1-C}{8\,C}\Bigl[\pi+2i\bigl(\omega\,\zeta(a)-\eta\,a\bigr)\Bigr],\] which is real since \(a\in i\mathbf{R}\). The third-kind term has coefficient \((1-C)/(8C)\), which vanishes only at \(C=1\), and the limit \(C\to1\) (\(\theta_0\to0\) or \(\Delta\phi\to0\)) gives \(I\to1\), i.e. \(\widetilde L_{81}\to4\alpha=8\Delta\theta\). For small \(D\) (i.e. \(C\), that is \(\Delta\theta\), small) the four terms of \(I(C)\) are individually of order \(1/C\) and cancel down to \[I(C)=\frac1{\sqrt C}\Bigl[1+\frac C4\Bigl(\ln\frac{16}{C}-\frac{11}{3}\Bigr)+O(C^2\ln C)\Bigr],\] equivalently \(\widetilde L_{81}=4\rho\bigl[1+\tfrac C4(\ln\tfrac{16}{C}-\tfrac{11}3)+\dots\bigr]\) with \(\rho:=\sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2}=\Delta\phi\sqrt{D^2+s^2}\), and in terms of the original parameters \[\widetilde L_{81}\simeq\Delta\phi\Bigl\{4s+\frac{2D^2}{s}\Bigl[\ln\frac{4s}{D}-\frac56\Bigr]\Bigr\}.\] Since \(\widetilde L_{81}-4\Delta\phi\,s\) is of order \(\Delta\theta^2\ln\Delta\theta\), which dominates the \(\Delta\theta^2\) term of the Taylor formula above, one obtains by adding the two the expansion of \(L_{81}\) itself up to order \(\Delta\theta^2\) on a logarithmic scale, \[L_{81}=4\Delta\phi\sin\theta_0+\frac{8\,\Delta\theta^2}{\Delta\phi\sin\theta_0}\Bigl[\ln\frac{2\Delta\phi\sin\theta_0}{\Delta\theta}-\frac56-\frac2{15}\Delta\phi^2\sin^2\theta_0\Bigr]+o(\Delta\theta^2),\] in which \(\widetilde L_{81}\) already accounts for the logarithmic term and the constant \(-5/6\), and the correction \(E''(0)\) only shifts the constant. For the values \(\Delta\theta=6\pi/180\), \(\Delta\phi=50\pi/180\) and \(\theta_0=65\pi/180\) one has \(D=0.24\), \(s\simeq0.90631\), \(\alpha\simeq0.20944\) and \(C\simeq0.065529<1/9\), so the lattice is rectangular, with \(g_2\simeq0.54445\), \(g_3\simeq-0.072409\), \(e_r\simeq0.25542\), \(\omega\simeq2.98311\), \(\omega'\simeq1.97382\,i\), \(\eta\simeq0.16719\), \(a\simeq-1.24106\,i\) and \(I(C)\simeq4.04218\). Hence \(\widetilde L_{81}=4\alpha I(C)\simeq3.38637\), while numerical quadrature of the original integral gives \(L_{81}\simeq3.37739\) (a relative difference of about \(-2.7\cdot10^{-3}\)), and the small-\(D\) expansion gives \(I\simeq4.0236\) and \(\widetilde L_{81}\simeq3.3708\). The Taylor correction is \(-\tfrac{16}{15}\Delta\phi\,s\,\Delta\theta^2\simeq-0.00925\), so that \(\widetilde L_{81}-\tfrac{16}{15}\Delta\phi\,s\,\Delta\theta^2\simeq3.37711\) matches \(L_{81}\) to \(8\cdot10^{-5}\) in relative value.
