2 Estimation of power take-off

A simple 1D dynamic model is derived in order to estimate the pto by the KEEP device, that is the mechanical power extracted from the rotation of the arm by the generator, modelled as a viscous torque \(-b\dot\alpha\) (average of \(b\dot\alpha^2\)). Significant assumptions are made. An \(\omega\)-pulsation harmonic couple around \(\vu z\), caused by the tether attached to the end of the arm, is modelled by \(C_M\,\cos(\omega\,t)\), where \(C_M=F_s\,a \sin \theta_0\) and \(F_s\) is a constant tension in the line. Thus, the variation of the aerodynamics forces along the kite’s figure-of-eight trajectory is averaged and projected onto the horizontal plane (\(z=0\)). The Newton’s second law for the arm (27) yields \begin{equation} \tag{37} I \ddot\alpha = C_M\,\cos(\omega\,t) -b \dot\alpha. \end{equation} The following non dimensional parameters \(\overline{b}=b/(I\,\omega)\), \(\overline{C_M}=C_M/(I\,\omega^2)\) and \(\tau=\omega\,t\) are defined. The derivative of \(\alpha\) with respect to \(\tau\) is denoted by \(\alpha'\), such that \(\alpha'=\dot{\alpha}/\omega\). The Newton’s second law for the arm (37) simplifies as follows \begin{equation} \tag{38} \alpha''+\overline{b}\, \alpha' = \overline{C_M}\,\cos(\tau). \end{equation} The steady-state harmonic solution of (38) is \begin{equation} \tag{39} \alpha = -\frac{\overline{C_M}}{B}\,\cos(\tau+\delta), \end{equation} where \(B=(1+\overline{b}^2)^{1/2}\) and \(\delta=\arctan \overline{b}\). This last relationship shows that the phase difference between the angular position of the arm and that of the kite is closely linked to the rotor resistance \(b\). The mechanical power produced by the device is expressed as \begin{equation} \tag{40} \dot{W} = b \, \dot \alpha^2=I\,\omega^3\,\overline{b}\,\alpha'^2. \end{equation} Consequently, the average power produced over a steady-state operating cycle of the KEEP system is given by \begin{equation} \tag{41} W_a = \frac{1}{2\pi}\,\int \limits_{\tau=0}^{\tau=2\pi} \dot{W} \dd{\tau}=\frac{\overline{b}}{2\,\left(1+\overline{b}^2\right)}\, \frac{{C_M}^2}{I\,\omega}. \end{equation} A maximum tension condition is imposed on the cable whilst seeking to preserve the structural integrity of the kite (maximum stress, local buckling of the inflatable tubes, etc.) (Desenclos et al. 2023). Denoting \(F_s\) the maximum tension, it is generally expressed as \(F_s=T_s\, S\), where \(T_s\) is a maximum wing loading independent of the kite’s surface area \(S\). Thus, the amplitude of the harmonic couple \(C_M\) can be expressed as follows: \begin{equation} \tag{42} C_M=T_s\, S\,a \sin(\theta_0). \end{equation} Furthermore, the pulsation \(\omega\) introduced in the simple formulation (37) can be determined using a zero-mass model of the kite (Leloup et al. 2014). We consider line length \(\ell\) that are much greater than the arm length \(a\), so that \(r=\ell\) is a reasonable assumption. Futhermore, assuming a constant kite speed \(V_k=\lVert \dot{\boldsymbol{r}} \rVert\) along its figure-of-eight trajectory, equal to the value at the center \((x,y,z)=(\ell\,\sin(\theta_0),0,\ell\,\cos(\theta_0))\), it can be shown that \begin{equation} \tag{43} V_k=w_\ell\,\left(\frac{\ell\,\cos(\theta_0)}{z_r}\right)^{1/7}\,\sqrt{\left(1+G^2\right)\,{\sin(\theta_0)}^2-1}, \end{equation} where \(G=C_L/C_D\) is the glide ratio of the kite. According to (3) and (4), the total length of the figure-of-eight trajectory \(L_{81}\) on the unit sphere (\(\ell=1\)) is given by \begin{equation} \tag{44} L_{81}=\int \limits_{\tau=0}^{\tau=2\pi} \sqrt{\left[2 \, \Delta\theta\, \cos(2\,\tau)\right]^2+{\sin(\theta_0 + \Delta\theta \sin(2\tau))}^2\,\left[\Delta\phi\, \cos(\tau)\right]^2} \dd{\tau}, \end{equation} hence the following expression of the pulsation \begin{equation} \tag{45} \omega=\frac{2\,\pi\,V_k}{\ell\,L_{81}}. \end{equation} As detailed in appendix C, one has the following estimate: \begin{equation} \tag{46} L_{81}=4\Delta\phi\sin\theta_0+\frac{8\,\Delta\theta^2}{\Delta\phi\sin\theta_0}\Bigl[\ln\frac{2\Delta\phi\sin\theta_0}{\Delta\theta}-\frac56-\frac2{15}\Delta\phi^2\sin^2\theta_0\Bigr]+o(\Delta\theta^2). \end{equation} Assuming that the values of the aerodynamics parameters \(G\), \(w_r\) and \(z_r\), of the geometry parameters \(a\), \(\ell\), \(\theta_0\), \(\Delta \theta\) and \(\Delta \phi\), as well as those of the kite properties \(S\) and \(T_s\), have been selected, the independent parameters \(b\) and \(I\) can be optimized. At fixed inertia \(I\), the last term of the relationship (41) reaches its maximum value for \(\overline{b}=1\), that is \begin{equation} \tag{47} b=I\,\omega, \end{equation} which gives the average power \(W_a = C_M^2/(4I\omega)\). For operating reasons, the magnitude of the angle \(\alpha\) is limited to \(\pi/2\). By (39), the amplitude is \(\overline{C_M}/B = C_M/(\omega\sqrt{I^2\omega^2+b^2})\), hence the condition \begin{equation} \tag{48} \omega^2\qty(I^2\omega^2 + b^2) \geqslant \qty(\frac{2\,C_M}{\pi})^2, \end{equation} which depends on both parameters. With \(Z = I^2\omega^2+b^2\), the power (41) reads \(W_a = b\,C_M^2/(2Z)\). At fixed \(Z\), it increases with \(b\), that is as \(I\) decreases, and at fixed \(b\) it decreases with \(Z\): in this model, the inertia of the arm only costs power, and the supremum \(\pi C_M\omega/4\) is approached as \(I\to0\), with (48) active. The inertia is bounded below by the structure of the arm, \(I\geq I_{\min}\), with \(I_{\min} = 62\,\mathrm{kg}\,\mathrm{m}^{2}\) the lower bound of Table 3 (a tube that withstands the maximum tension of the lines). As long as \(I_{\min} < \sqrt2\,C_M/(\pi\omega^2)\), which holds here, the condition (48) is active at the optimum, and the parameters \(b\) and \(I\) are optimal when \begin{equation} \tag{49} I = I_{\min}, \qquad b = \frac{2\,C_M}{\pi\,\omega}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}, \qquad \varepsilon = \frac{\pi\,\omega^2 I_{\min}}{2\,C_M}, \end{equation} where \(C_M\) and \(\omega\) are given by (42) and (45) respectively, which leads to the following average power: \begin{equation} \tag{50} W_a =\frac{\pi\,C_M\,\omega}{4}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}. \end{equation} The design \(b = I\omega\) with the smallest admissible \(I\), \(I = \sqrt2\,C_M/(\pi\omega^2) = 646\,\mathrm{kg}\,\mathrm{m}^{2}\), satisfies the constraint but gives only \(\pi C_M\omega/(4\sqrt2)\): the condition (47) is optimal at fixed \(I\), not jointly, and the optimum (49) gives \(41\,\%\) more power. Thus, the power take-off per unit area of kite \(pto\) by the KEEP device can be estimated as follows: \begin{equation} \tag{51} pto=\frac{W_a}{S}=\left(\frac{\pi^2\,\sin(\theta_0)\,\sqrt{\left(1+G^2\right)\,{\sin(\theta_0)}^2-1}}{2\,L_{81}}\right)\,\left(\frac{T_s\,a\,w_r}{\ell}\right)\,\left(\frac{\ell\,\cos(\theta_0)}{z_r}\right)^{1/7}\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}. \end{equation} The maximum power take-off varies almost linearly with the glide ratio of the kite. The linear dependence of \(pto\) on wind speed \(w_r\) is due to the structural constraint \(T_s\). Without this constraint, the dependence of \(pto\) on wind speed \(w_r\) is normally of the third order, since the aerodynamic pressures – and therefore the wing loading – are of the second order. The \(pto\) appears to increase linearly as the arm length \(a\) increases. The shorter the length of the line \(\ell\), the higher \(pto\) is, even if the wind speed at the height of the kite decreases. However, the inertia of the kite is not taken into account in this estimate of the power take-off. Taking it into account could alter the value of the pulsation \(\omega\) and, consequently, lead to an optimal value for the length of the line. Taking the nominal parameters of Table 1 with \(T_s = 1000\,\mathrm{N}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\), \(\varepsilon = 0.068\), and the KEEP device achieves a maximum power take-off of about \(5.0\,\mathrm{kW}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\) (5.1 kW m−2 as \(I_{\min}\to0\)), regardless of whether expression (44) or (46) is used for \(L_{81}\). This estimate assumes a glide ratio \(G = 7\) (Table 1); with \(G = 5\), a value more typical of soft kites, it drops to \(3.6\,\mathrm{kW}\,\mathrm{m}^{-2}\).

function param_table(p)
    # symbol, description, unit, optional value override
    params = [
        Parameter(:a, "a", "Arm length", raw"\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:l, raw"\ell", "Line length", raw"\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:m, "m", "Kite mass", raw"\kg", nothing),
        Parameter(:I, "I", "Arm inertia", raw"\kg\square\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:ρ_a, raw"\rho_a", "Air density", raw"\kg\per\cubic\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:d_l, raw"d_\ell", "Line diameter", raw"\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:S, "S", "Kite surface", raw"\square\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:C_L, "C_L", "Kite lift coefficient", "", nothing),
        Parameter(:C_D, "C_D", "Kite drag coefficient", "", "C_L / 7"),
        Parameter(:C_D_l, raw"C_{D,\ell}", "Line drag coefficient", "", nothing),
        Parameter(:ρ_l, raw"\rho_\ell", "Line density", raw"\kg\per\cubic\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:g, "g", "Gravity", raw"\meter\per\square\second", nothing),
        Parameter(:z0, "z_0", "Wind reference altitude", raw"\meter", nothing),
        Parameter(:w0, "w_0", "Wind reference speed", raw"\meter\per\second", nothing),
        Parameter(:b, "b", "Arm braking coefficient",
            raw"\newton\meter\second\per\degree", f"{p.b / rad2deg(1):.4g}"),  # 4 digits: fixed point b = I ω
        Parameter(:θ0, raw"\theta_0", "Lemniscate mean polar angle",
            raw"\degree", f"{rad2deg(p.θ0):.0f}"),
        Parameter(:Δθ, raw"\Delta\theta", "Lemniscate polar half-height",
            raw"\degree", f"{rad2deg(p.Δθ):.0f}"),
        Parameter(:Δφ, raw"\Delta\phi", "Lemniscate azimuthal half-width",
            raw"\degree", f"{rad2deg(p.Δφ):.0f}"),
    ]

    sort!(params, by=x -> eor_transform(x.sym))
    table_rows = map(params) do p_item
        val = isnothing(p_item.value_override) ? f"{p[p_item.key]:.9g}" : p_item.value_override
        unit_col = isempty(p_item.unit) ? "" : raw"\si{" * p_item.unit * "}"
        f"    ${p_item.sym}$ & {p_item.descr} & ${val}$ & {unit_col} \\\\ "
    end


    table_str = raw"""
\begin{table}[thpb]
\centering
\caption{Parameter Description and Values}
\label{tab:params}
\begin{tabular}{l p{3.2cm} r l}
    \toprule
    {Symbol} & {Description} & {Value} & {Unit} \\
    \midrule
""" * join(table_rows, "\n") * raw"""

    \bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}"""
    return table_str
end
